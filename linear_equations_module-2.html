⚖️ The golden rule
A linear equation states that two expressions are equal. The key rule is: whatever you do to one side, you must do the same to the other. Think of it as a perfectly balanced scale — if you add or remove something from one side without doing the same to the other, the balance is broken. Your goal is always to isolate x — get the variable by itself on one side.

🔑 Key vocabulary
📌 Variable
A letter (usually x) representing an unknown number we are trying to find.
📌 Equation
A mathematical statement with an equals sign (=) showing two expressions are equal.
⚡ Inverse operation
The opposite operation used to undo: + ↔ − and × ↔ ÷
⚡ Substitution
Replacing the variable with your answer to verify the equation is true.
📋 4-step strategy for solving any linear equation
  • Read carefully. Identify the variable and which operations have been applied to it.
  • Apply inverse operations. Undo operations in reverse order to isolate x. Apply the same operation to BOTH sides every time.
  • Show every step. Write each line of working clearly. Do not skip steps — each step is worth marks.
  • Check by substitution. Replace x with your answer in the original equation. If both sides are equal, your solution is correct.
🌍 Linear equations in real life
Linear equations appear everywhere. Here are three real examples:
🛒
Shopping
You have $50 and spend $8 per item. How many items can you buy if $2 remains?
50 − 8x = 2
🚴
Travel
A cyclist travels at 12 km/h. How many hours to cover 36 km?
12x = 36
🌡️
Temperature
Convert 20°C to Fahrenheit using F = 1.8C + 32.
F = 1.8(20) + 32

📈 What does a linear equation look like on a graph? (ACMNA193)
Every linear equation can be shown as a straight line on the Cartesian plane. When we solve 2x + 1 = 7, we are finding the x-value where the line y = 2x + 1 meets the horizontal line y = 7.
The graph confirms algebraically that when x = 3: left side = 2(3)+1 = 7 = right side ✓

    Differentiated learning pathways
    👁️ Visual learners
    Start with the balance scale in the Explore tab. Move the slider and watch what happens before writing any algebra. The visual model is your anchor for the abstract concept.
    Foundation
    📋 Step-by-step scaffold
    Work through all five examples in the Examples tab in order. Cover the reason column and try to identify each step yourself before revealing it.
    Foundation
    ✏️ 10-question practice
    Questions in the Practice tab are graded: Q1–4 are one-step (easy 🟢), Q5–8 are two-step (medium 🟡), and Q9–10 are challenge level (hard 🔴). Read each explanation carefully.
    Core
    📈 Graphical connection
    For any equation you solve, plot y = left side and y = right side on graph paper. Where they intersect gives your answer visually — linking algebra to ACMNA193.
    Core
    ⚡ Extension: write your own
    Create a word problem whose solution requires solving a linear equation. Swap with a classmate. Try equations with negative numbers, fractions, or unknowns on both sides.
    Extension
    🌍 Real-world modelling
    Choose one of the real-world examples from the Learn tab. Write your own similar scenario, set up the equation, solve it algebraically, and verify graphically.
    Extension
    📬 A note for parents and home learners

    This module is designed to be used independently at home. The most powerful support you can offer is to ask your child to explain each step to you aloud — even if you already know the answer. Teaching someone else consolidates understanding deeply. If they get stuck, the balance scale and worked examples are always available. Encourage them to check every answer by substitution: it builds the self-correction habit that strong mathematicians use.
