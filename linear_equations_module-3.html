⚖️ What is a linear equation?
A linear equation is a mathematical statement that two expressions are equal. It always involves a variable (usually x) raised to the power of 1 — no squares, no cubes.

The most important rule: whatever you do to one side, you must do to the other. Think of a perfectly balanced scale — if you add weight to one side, you must add the same to the other to keep it level.
✅ ACMNA194 — Solve linear equations using algebraic and graphical techniques
🔍
Identify the unknown
Find the variable (x, y, n…). Your goal is to get it alone on one side of the equals sign.
🔄
Inverse operations
Use the opposite operation to undo what's applied to x. Addition ↔ Subtraction. Multiplication ↔ Division.
⚖️
Maintain balance
Every operation must be applied to both sides equally. This is the golden rule — never break it.
✅
Verify by substitution
Always check: substitute your answer back into the original equation and confirm both sides are equal.
💡 Common mistakes to avoid
  • Forgetting to apply the same operation to both sides
  • Dividing by the wrong number — always divide by the coefficient of x
  • Sign errors when moving terms across the equals sign
  • Skipping the substitution check — always verify your answer!

🌍 Real-world connections
Linear equations appear everywhere in daily life:

🛒 Shopping: "A shirt costs $5 more than twice the price of a hat. If the shirt costs $35, what does the hat cost?" → 2x + 5 = 35

🚗 Travel: "A car travels at a constant speed. After 3 hours it has covered 210 km. What is the speed?" → 3x = 210

🌡 Temperature: "Converting Celsius to Fahrenheit: F = 9/5 × C + 32. If F = 77°, find C." → 9/5x + 32 = 77

Differentiated learning pathways
🌱 Foundation pathway
For students who need concrete support before abstract algebra.
  • Start with the balance scale visualiser (Tab 3)
  • Work through Examples 1 & 2 only (Tab 2)
  • Attempt Quiz Questions 1–4 (one-step)
  • Use worked examples as a reference while answering
🎯 Core pathway
For students working at Year 8 level expectations.
  • Read all of Tab 1 (Learn)
  • Work through Examples 1–4 (Tab 2)
  • Complete all 10 quiz questions
  • Review any incorrect answers carefully
🚀 Extension pathway
For students ready to go beyond the core curriculum.
  • Complete all 10 questions with 100% accuracy
  • Work through Example 5 — variables on both sides
  • Create your own real-world word problems
  • Research: can a linear equation have no solution or infinitely many solutions?
📬 A note for parents and home learners

This module works well at home — no specialist maths knowledge is needed from you as a parent. The most effective support you can provide is to ask your child to explain each solution step in their own words. Research shows that teaching someone else is one of the most powerful consolidation strategies. If they get stuck, the worked examples (Tab 2) and the balance scale visualiser (Tab 3) are always available to revisit.
